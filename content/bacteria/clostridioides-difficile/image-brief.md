**1. Slug (ASCII, language-neutral):** `clostridioides-difficile`

**2. Article title + one-line summary:** "Carrying C. difficile can lower your risk of C. difficile" — a spore-forming gut organism that turns dangerous when antibiotics strip away the neighbours holding it down; silently carrying it went with less diarrhoea, not more.

**3. Organism / structure in the preview:** *Clostridioides difficile* among the ordinary residents of the large intestine, with the dormant spore form visible.

**4. Morphology, in words (truth — do not invent the shape):** Gram-positive, slender, straight to slightly curved rods, roughly three to six times longer than they are wide, single or in pairs and short chains; anaerobic, so no oxygen-world cue. The defining feature is the spore: in some cells an oval, thick-walled body forms near one end and visibly swells that end, giving the rod a club or drumstick outline. A few free spores lie loose in the field, smooth and closed, plainly tougher-looking than the living rods. Around them a mixed crowd of other gut residents — shorter rods, a few small cocci — clearly more numerous than the difficile cells. No branching Y-shapes, no spirals, no spheres in place of the rods.

**5. Preview mood (one line, warm science):** A quiet, crowded micro-world — a slender navy rod with one swollen end, sitting hemmed in by teal and green neighbours; the field feels full, not menacing, and only two or three sealed spores carry a harder edge, as if waiting. One plane of focus, everything else softly falling away. Light and airy, never a dark plate with a cold glow. Light and accent as in Marika's slot-and-focus block below (2026-09-16).

**6. Hero mechanism (the answer, one clause) — Magnus, 2026-09-16:** *C. difficile* waits as dormant spores held in check by the gut's residents; when antibiotics strip those residents away, the spores wake, the rods multiply and their toxins loosen the seal of the gut lining.

**7. Three beats (first → next → outcome):** (1) Slender drumstick-ended rods and sealed spores sit hemmed in by a dense, varied crowd of residents, with a fine mist of the bile acids those residents make hanging among them; (2) the crowd is gone — most residents faded to empty husks, the mist cleared — and the sealed spores lie in the open (antibiotics shown only as that absence, never as a pill); (3) outcome: the spores open into slender rods that multiply in a spreading group, and beneath them the tight seams between the lining cells slacken and small gaps open — clean and calm, no blood, no wound.

**8. The one number (keyFacts, source id):** none shown — clause dropped. **15. The single idea both frames must carry:** Carrying is not the same as having — this organism is quiet while it is surrounded, and dangerous once the surroundings are gone. The card shows it hemmed in by neighbours; the article image shows the brake coming off.
