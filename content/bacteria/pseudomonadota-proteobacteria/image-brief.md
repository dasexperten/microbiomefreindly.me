## Editorial notes for Lisa, Otto and Marika (not prompt text)

**The single idea both images must carry.** Presence is normal; the *level* carries the information. Both frames say "a few more than there should be, in the one place where oxygen reaches". Neither image should say "dangerous bacteria". The article's own line: the reading is a smoke alarm, not the fire.

**The mechanism in one sentence.** The healthy gut lining keeps the colon almost free of oxygen, which suits the strict anaerobes that dominate it; when inflammation or antibiotics let oxygen leak in at the lining, the Pseudomonadota — which can breathe oxygen while their neighbours cannot — grow on that difference and their share rises.

**Priority.** Bacteria entry = evergreen. No launch date pressure; truth of the shape (field 4) outranks speed.

**Colour, in brand words.** Ground warm greige to paper; rods navy with teal; other bacteria in charcoal and warm grey. One accent per frame, on the oxygen — not a wash, not a glow. No violet.

**Scale cue.** The rounded cells of the lining behind the rods are the only sense of size the card frame needs — no scale bar, no label. Keep the rods inside the middle band of that frame so the og crop loses only air (Marika §1).

**Two masters, two worlds.** The built infographic replaces the 2026-09-02 bench scene: no person in either frame. They share the palette and the idea, nothing else.
