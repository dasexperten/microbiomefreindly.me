## Editorial notes for Lisa and Marika (not prompt text)

**The single idea both images must carry.** Presence is normal; the *level* carries the information. The preview shows "a few more than there should be, in the one place where oxygen reaches"; the hero shows a scientist reading that quantity calmly. Neither image should say "dangerous bacteria". The article's own line: the reading is a smoke alarm, not the fire.

**The mechanism in one sentence.** The healthy gut lining keeps the colon almost free of oxygen, which suits the strict anaerobes that dominate it; when inflammation or antibiotics let oxygen leak in at the lining, the Pseudomonadota — which can breathe oxygen while their neighbours cannot — grow on that difference and their share rises.

**File name keyword (Jurgen's pattern).** `pseudomonadota-proteobacteria-preview` · `pseudomonadota-proteobacteria-hero` (plus `-og`, `-thumb` per Marika's table). Encyclopedia path: `mbf/bacteria/pseudomonadota-proteobacteria/`.

**Priority.** Bacteria entry = evergreen. No launch date pressure; truth of the shape (field 4) outranks speed.

**Colour, in brand words.** Ground ivory; rods navy with teal; lining surface in a soft green-teal. One accent only: a small gold note where the light meets the surface, standing in for the oxygen — not a wash, not a glow. Violet is not needed here.

**Scale cue for the preview.** The folds of the lining behind the rods are the only sense of size the frame needs — no scale bar, no label. Keep the rods inside the middle band of the frame so the og crop loses only air (Marika §1).

**Two masters, two worlds.** The hero bench is a room with a person; the preview is the inside of the gut lining. They share the palette and the idea, nothing else.
