- **The mechanism in one sentence, for Lisa's understanding:** E. coli is the gut's main oxygen-tolerant bacterium — it can breathe air when there is a trace and ferment when there is none — so it sits near the gut lining where oxygen seeps in, uses it up, and is thought to keep the colon airless for the strict anaerobes that make up most of the microbiome; the harm comes only from a minority of strains that carry toxin or attachment genes and end up in the wrong place (bladder, blood).
- **Truth check for field 4:** the shape is the same for the resident and the outbreak strains; the difference is genes, not appearance. So the preview is honest as long as the rods are rods. The colonies on the hero plate are small, round, smooth and pale — nothing coloured, nothing exotic.
