## Notes for Marika and Lisa (plain words, not prompt text)

- **The single idea both images carry:** the ordinary resident. Not the outbreak, not the headline — the quiet neighbour that has lived in the colon for years. The card shows it at home; the article image shows the place it holds, next to the lining, without alarm.
- **Maya consult:** none needed — no product and no health claim sits next to either image.
- **The mechanism in one sentence, for Lisa's understanding:** E. coli is the gut's main oxygen-tolerant bacterium — it can breathe air when there is a trace and ferment when there is none — so it sits near the gut lining where oxygen seeps in, uses it up, and is thought to keep the colon airless for the strict anaerobes that make up most of the microbiome; the harm comes only from a minority of strains that carry toxin or attachment genes and end up in the wrong place (bladder, blood).
- **Truth check for field 4:** the shape is the same for the resident and the outbreak strains; the difference is genes, not appearance. So both frames are honest as long as the rods are rods and the threads spread around the whole body.
