## The single idea both images must carry

Where a microbe lives matters as much as which microbe it is. In the mouth this organism is a builder — the beam that holds the plaque community together — not an intruder. Both frames should feel calm, orderly and warm; neither frame should look like a threat.

## For Lisa and Otto — the mechanism in one sentence

Fusobacterium nucleatum is the connector of dental plaque: its long spindle-shaped cells can stick to many different kinds of bacteria at the same time, so it bridges the early colonisers and the late ones into one stable, ordered community — and only when one particular sub-lineage of it travels down to the colon does it become the organism the headlines are afraid of. That second half is told in words, never pictured.
