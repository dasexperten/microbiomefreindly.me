Streptococcus mutans is the bacterium most people mean when they say "the cavity germ." It's a chain-forming coccus that lives in the plaque on your teeth, and it's been studied for a century, so let me tell you what the evidence actually says, and where it runs out.

It was named in 1924 by a bacteriologist called Clarke, who grew it from a decayed tooth. The name hasn't changed since. What did change, in 2021, was the phylum it's filed under: Firmicutes became Bacillota. Same bug, new label.

You weren't born with it. It needs a tooth to hold on to.
