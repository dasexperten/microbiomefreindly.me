You asked whether your gut comes back after antibiotics. Mostly, yes. And "mostly" is doing real work in that sentence.

Start with the clearest human study. Palleja and colleagues, Nature Microbiology, twenty eighteen. Twelve healthy young men took a four day cocktail of three last resort antibiotics — meropenem, gentamicin and vancomycin — and gave stool samples for the next six months. This is a worst case on purpose. Nobody gets that combination for a sore throat.

The first thing that happened was not emptiness. It was a reshuffle. Enterobacteria bloomed. Bifidobacterium fell. So did the bacteria that make butyrate, the fuel your colon cells burn.

Then the timeline. Within about one and a half months, the community was back near where it started. That is the good half. The other half is that nine species present in every one of those twelve men before treatment were still undetectable in most of them at day one hundred and eighty.

Dethlefsen and Relman followed three people through two courses of ciprofloxacin for ten months. Diversity fell within three to four days of the first dose. Each community began coming back about a week after each course, but the return was often incomplete, and every gut settled into a composition that was not the original one.

And the long tail is long. Jernberg and colleagues followed four people for two years after a single seven day course of clindamycin. Their Bacteroides never returned to its original composition inside that window.

Now, what helps. Probiotics have good evidence for one thing: diarrhoea. A Cochrane review of thirty three trials in children, six thousand three hundred and fifty two participants, found antibiotic associated diarrhoea in eight percent on probiotics against nineteen percent on control. Nine children treated to prevent one case.

But that is a symptom, not a rebuild. In the one study that sampled the gut lining directly, a probiotic actually delayed the return of a person's own microbiome, compared with simply waiting.

So: finish the course. Give it months, not days. Eat fibre. And if anyone offers to rebuild your microbiome on a schedule, ask them which study measured it.
