## 2. The mechanism in one clause (from the `answer` field)

Two people did not run the same experiment: the effect depends on the exact strain — the named variety, not just the species — so preparations that look identical do different things.

## 3. The organism or structure, with its real morphology in plain words

The subject is **lactobacilli — the same species, different strains** — and the gut surface they meet. The whole argument of the piece is that these bodies are indistinguishable by eye and are not the same organism, so the frame must show cells that genuinely look alike.

- **The cells: rods.** *Lactobacillus* and *Lactiplantibacillus* are Gram-positive rods — straight to slightly curved, blunt-ended, a few micrometres long. Grains of rice, not spheres. They lie single, in pairs, or in short end-to-end chains. **No spores. No flagella. No branching, no Y-fork, no spiral, no filament.** A round *Lactobacillus* is a factual error and the frame goes back (morphology is truth — `docs/IMAGE_PROMPT.md`, rule 3).
- **The named ones in the article** are all of this same shape: *L. rhamnosus* GG, *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*, *L. helveticus* R0175. They differ by strain code, not by silhouette. That is the point — do not invent a visual difference between them, because in reality there is none to see.
- **The ground they meet:** a soft, semi-transparent mucus film lying over the gut lining, and beneath it the lining rising in rounded, finger-shaped folds. A living wet surface, not a hard crust and not a tunnel.

The eleven-strain mixture in the Zmora study is **not** given a cast in this frame: our article does not name its species, so nothing may be drawn for it. Nothing enters the picture that the text cannot vouch for.

## 4. The three-beat process — first, next, outcome

From the article's H2 skeleton, read as one path:

1. **First — six groups that look the same.** Six small clusters of rods arrive above the mucus film. Same shape, same size, same posture, same colour. Nothing on any surface distinguishes one cluster from another. This is "probiotic is a category, not a specification": a reader looking at beat one should genuinely be unable to tell them apart.
2. **Next — each cluster meets the gut surface.** The six settle onto the same mucus film over the same lining. The setting is identical for all of them; only the identity of the cells differs, and that identity is invisible.
3. **Outcome — four of the six change what they touch; two leave it exactly as it was.** Under four clusters the mucus and the community in it visibly respond — the field around them shifts. Under the other two, the film is untouched: the same texture, the same arrangement, as if nothing had been placed there at all. Same species, same setting, two outcomes.

The eye must walk **identical → same room → four act, two do not** in one pass, and arrive at the third beat understanding that the sameness in beat one was never real sameness.

## 5. The single sourced number, shown by scale or quantity

**Four of six.**
Of the six *Lactobacillus*-containing probiotic types pooled for prevention of antibiotic-associated diarrhoea in adults, only four showed significant efficacy — and *L. rhamnosus* GG was one of the two that did not.
Source: McFarland L. V., Evans C. T., Goldstein E. J. C., *Frontiers in Medicine*, 2018 — doi `10.3389/fmed.2018.00124`, PMID `29868585` (article source `s3`; record confirmed against PubMed on 2026-09-04, https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2018.00124).

Show it as **quantity, never as a printed figure**: six clusters in the frame, four of them visibly acting on the film beneath, two visibly not. Six and four, exactly — countable at a glance, not a suggestion of "most" or "some". No percentage sign, no ratio, no digit anywhere in the pixels. The count is the whole of the number.

No second number goes into the image. Every other figure in the piece — the 1.98 kg, the 30 × 10⁹ CFU, the 12 weeks, the 1–2 days — stays in the text where its source marker can follow it.
