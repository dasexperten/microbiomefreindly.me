**2. The mechanism in one clause (from the `answer` field)**

Spore-formers travel as tough dormant seeds and survive the acid by construction while delicate cells mostly do not — and a strain that survives the trip may still never settle on the gut lining, because passing through is not moving in.

**3. The organism or structure, with its real morphology in plain words**

Not one species — four kinds of traveller and the two surfaces they cross. Every shape below is the organism's ordinary textbook form and is named in the article; nothing here is invented, and nothing may be redrawn as something else:

- **Slender straight rods with rounded ends, single or in short chains** — *Lactobacillus plantarum* IS-10506, the delicate traveller. A living, metabolising cell with a membrane to defend. Never spheres, never branched, never spiral.
- **Small oval cocci in pairs and short chains of four to six** — *Enterococcus faecium* IS-27526. Round-to-egg-shaped, two together or a short row, never a long chain, never a rod.
- **A rod carrying one oval spore at its end, and free spores loose beside it** — *Heyndrickxia coagulans* LMG S-24828, still labelled by its older name *Bacillus coagulans*. The spore is an oval body with thick layered coats, slightly swelling the end of the mother cell, and it is visibly a different object from a living cell: dense, dehydrated, armoured, no membrane detail. This is the piece's whole point about spores — it must read as a seed, not as a small bacterium.
- **A cluster of those slender rods held inside a thin translucent shell** — a microencapsulated dose. Cell-scale: the shell hugs a handful of cells the way a bead of gel would, a few cell-widths thick. It is **not** a swallowable capsule, a gel-cap, a tablet or anything a person could hold.
- **The acid field** — the stomach side: a dense, crowded, hostile ground the travellers are moving through. An environment, not an organ; no anatomical stomach silhouette, no cut-away body diagram.
- **The lining on the far side** — the gut wall rising in a rank of finger-shaped folds under a soft translucent mucus film. Cells settle *into* that film, not onto a bare surface.

No filaments, no biofilm sheet, no spirals, no forked Y-shaped cells: none of those belong in this frame.

**4. The three-beat process — first, next, outcome**

1. **First:** the swallowed mixed crowd enters the acid field together — slender rods, ovoid pairs, spore-bearing rods with their loose spores, and one shelled cluster. Dense, varied, all still whole.
2. **Next:** the acid gate. Most of the free rods and ovoid pairs come apart — slumped, emptied husks, losing their outline; not gore, just structures that have collapsed. The oval spores pass through unchanged, exactly as they went in. The shelled cluster passes with its rods intact inside it.
3. **Outcome:** on the far side the survivors reach the folded, mucus-covered lining — and they do two different things. Some sink into the mucus between the folds and take hold. Others drift on along the wall without ever attaching. Both must be visible at once, because that is the answer: arriving and settling are not the same event.

The eye must be able to walk that path in one pass: crowded → filtered → some settled, some still passing.

**5. The single sourced number, shown by scale or quantity**

Shown as **proportion in the delicate lane**: of every twenty free rods that enter the acid field, fewer than four come out the far side still whole — countable without effort, a wide gap between what went in and what emerged. Nothing else in the frame carries a number. The shelled cluster may show its cells surviving, but no quantity is attached to it and none may be implied by matching counts. The number is never printed, never labelled, never written anywhere in the pixels. Proportion is the whole of it.
