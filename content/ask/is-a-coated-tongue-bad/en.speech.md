A reader looked in the mirror, saw a pale film across the back of the tongue, and asked whether that means something is wrong. Let me answer first and explain after. For most people, most of the time, it means nothing is wrong.

Here is what that film actually is. Bollen and Beikler, in the International Journal of Oral Science, describe the top of the tongue as about twenty-five square centimetres of irregular surface, and they list what collects there: food remnants, shed skin cells from your own mouth, and anaerobic bacteria. Read that list again. Most of what a scraper lifts off your tongue is you. Your own dead surface cells, plus the community that grows on them.

That community is large. Bordas and colleagues sampled the tongues of nineteen volunteers and found between one million and one hundred million bacteria per sample. Bernardi and colleagues, using two methods together, counted more than eighty different species. A coated tongue is a habitat, and habitats are supposed to be occupied.

The smell is chemistry, not dirt. Bacteria break down amino acids from those shed cells. Cysteine becomes hydrogen sulphide. Methionine becomes methyl mercaptan. Those are the volatile sulphur compounds someone standing close to you notices.

Now, when is it normal? Van Gils and colleagues scored the tongues of two hundred and sixty-eight healthy young adults. A thin white coating was the highest score recorded in ninety-three per cent of the men and eighty-seven per cent of the women. That is the ordinary finding, not the exception.

When does it point at something? When a white layer wipes off and leaves raw, red tissue underneath. When the coating is one-sided, painful, ulcerated or bleeding. When the papillae look elongated and brown or black. And when it arrives with a dry mouth. In a study of fifty-three older adults, reduced saliva went with a heavier coating and with roughly four times the odds of Candida in the mouth.

And what actually reduces it? Cleaning the tongue, gently, every day. The Cochrane review calls the evidence very low certainty and the effect small. In one crossover study, only daily scraping lowered the next morning's bacterial load. Probiotics improved judged breath scores but did not change the coating at all.

So: clean it once a day, expect a modest effect, and do not chase a pink tongue.
