## 2. The mechanism in one clause (from `answer`)

Shed cells from the mouth's own surface, food remnants and anaerobic bacteria are held between the papillae — the tiny bumps that give the tongue its rough surface.

## 3. The organism and structure, real morphology in plain words

The frame is built on the **tongue dorsum surface itself**, not on a single cell:

- **Papillae** — a dense field of small, blunt, keratinised bumps standing close together, taller and more crowded toward the back of the tongue, with narrow valleys between them. They are bumps and short spikes, never smooth, never flat, never regular like a grid.
- **Shed epithelial cells** — thin, flattened, irregular flakes with curled edges, larger than any bacterium, lying loose in the valleys between the papillae. This is the bulk of the material; the picture must read as *mostly flakes*, not mostly microbes.
- **Bacteria among them**, three real shapes, each true to what the article names:
  - **spheres in short chains and pairs** — *Streptococcus salivarius*, *Rothia mucilaginosa*;
  - **slender branching filaments and short rods** — *Actinomyces*-type cells, forked and thread-like;
  - **plain straight rods** — the mixed anaerobes.
  No spirals, no spores, no flagella tails, no cocci where the text names filaments and none the other way round.
- Optionally a **thin, translucent film of saliva** over the field — a wet sheen, not a thick gel dome.

## 4. The three-beat process (from the H2 skeleton)

1. **First** — the valleys between the papillae fill: flakes of shed surface cells and food remnants settle and are held there, with bacteria settling on them.
2. **Next** — the anaerobic cells, packed in the low-oxygen bottom of those valleys, break down the sulphur-bearing peptides in that debris.
3. **Outcome** — volatile sulphur compounds leave the surface: the smell. The coating itself stays a thin layer at the back and, in most healthy mouths, stays ordinary.

The eye should read the picture along that path — deep valley floor → packed cells → what rises off the surface — in one movement.

## 5. The single sourced number, shown by scale or quantity

**90.7 % — a thin coating, in 268 systemically healthy young adults, was the heaviest thickness score recorded for 90.7 % of them; only 7.1 % reached a thick coating anywhere on the tongue.** Source `s2` (Van Gils L.M. et al., *International Journal of Dental Hygiene*, 2020 · doi 10.1111/idh.12416 · PMID 31309703).

Show it as **proportion, never as a printed number**: the overwhelming majority of the field carries only a thin layer sitting low between the papillae, and a single small region carries a visibly thicker one. Roughly nine parts thin to one part thick, read at a glance: across a row of ten valleys, nine hold only a thin low layer and exactly one holds a visibly thicker one. If proportion cannot be made to read cleanly, drop the clause — do not print the figure and do not substitute another number.
