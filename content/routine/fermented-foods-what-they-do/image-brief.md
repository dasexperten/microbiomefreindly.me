**2. The mechanism in one clause (from the `answer` field)**

The live lactic acid bacteria swallowed with kefir, yoghurt, kimchi and sauerkraut mostly do not settle in the gut; the resident community shifts instead, and with it gut microbial diversity rises and 19 inflammatory blood proteins fall.

**3. The organism or structure, with its real morphology in plain words**

Two populations that must read as visibly different kinds of cell, plus the surface they meet on. Every shape below is either named in the sources behind this piece or is that organism's ordinary textbook form. Nothing is invented, and nothing may be redrawn as something else.

**The arrivals — lactic acid bacteria of the four foods:**

- **Long slender rods with rounded ends** — *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, one of the two organisms yoghurt is defined by. Straight, not curved, noticeably longer than they are wide.
- **Round cells in pairs and in chains** — *Streptococcus thermophilus*, the other defining yoghurt organism. Spheres that touch in a line of four to eight, never a solid clump.
- **Short plump rods and small ovoid cells in pairs and short chains** — the kefir community: *Lentilactobacillus kefiri* with *Leuconostoc*, *Lactococcus* and *Acetobacter* species, as the Codex standard for kefir describes it. Ordinary bacterial size, no ornament.
- **Large oval budding cells** — the lactose-fermenting and non-lactose-fermenting yeasts of kefir. Clearly bigger than every bacterium in the frame, each one with a smaller daughter cell swelling from its side. This size difference is real and should be visible.
- **Mixed rods and round cells on ragged plant cell-wall material** — the wild lactic acid bacteria of kimchi and sauerkraut, which are whatever grew on the cabbage. No species is named for these two in the piece, so none may be implied by giving them a distinctive shape of their own.

**The residents — the community already living in the gut:**

- **Plain straight rods with rounded ends**, in many lengths and thicknesses, some with fine hair-like tails, most without, lying in and under a thin translucent mucus film over the gut lining; beneath the film the lining rises in a rank of finger-shaped folds. No gut genus is named in this article, so no resident cell may be drawn as a recognisable named species: they are an ordinary mixed community, varied in shape, anonymous by design.

**The surfaces:** a pale open lattice of plant cell-wall strands and ragged sheets (what is left of cabbage after chewing and stomach acid), and, for the milk ferments, a soft translucent matrix rather than a liquid pool. No glass, no vessel, no rim, no lid anywhere — see field 8.

The two populations must be told apart by shape and by nothing else — never by making the arrivals brighter, larger or more saturated. They are not the heroes of this frame; that is the whole point of the article.

No spores swelling a cell end, no spirals, no filaments, no forked or Y-shaped cells, no biofilm sheet, no capsule-shaped body: none of those belong here.

**4. The three-beat process — first, next, outcome**

1. **First — what is alive in the ferment.** A dense, crowded field of the arrival cells: the long rods, the chains of spheres, the plump kefir rods with their few big budding yeasts among them, packed on plant fibre and in the milk matrix. Abundance is the state the reader starts from, and it should look genuinely full.
2. **Next — the arrivals pass through, and mostly do not stay.** The same cells now moving across the resident field: drifting over and past the mucus and the resident rods, carried along rather than settling, only a single one of them taking a place in the community. Alongside them, and just as much a part of this beat, the invisible half of the food arriving daily — organic acids, peptides and broken-down sugars, shown as a soft chemical drift through the mucus, never as written symbols or formulae. This is the working middle of the frame and carries the most detail.
3. **Outcome — the resident community itself changes.** The gut field ends more varied than it began: more different resident shapes than in beat two, drawn from the residents themselves, not from the arrivals. The change belongs to the cells that were already there.

The eye must walk that path in one pass: a crowded ferment → cells drifting past a resident field and not settling → a resident field that has become more varied on its own.

**5. The single sourced number, shown by scale or quantity**

**5.4 %.** The overlap between the new bacterial types appearing in people's guts and the bacterial types in the fermented foods they were eating peaked at 5.4 % early in the intervention, and at later time points was no different from the high-fibre arm — Wastyk H. C. et al., *Cell*, 2021 (`s1` in the article, doi 10.1016/j.cell.2021.06.019, PMID 34256014).

Shown as **proportion in beat two**: of roughly twenty newly arrived cells crossing the resident field, exactly one takes a place among the residents and the rest travel on. About one in twenty is the honest visual reading of 5.4 % at its highest point; the picture shows the smallness of the share, not a printed figure. The number is never printed, never labelled, never written anywhere in the pixels, and the frame must not imply a precise percentage — quantity and proportion are the whole of it.

**8. What must never appear**

No jar, glass, tub, pot, bottle, carton, lid, spoon, bowl or plate — nothing that reads as packaging or as a container, and no product, capsule, tablet, sachet or brand mark of any kind. No kitchen and no food photography: no recognisable cabbage, cucumber, milk pour, yoghurt swirl or kimchi; the foods appear only as plant cell-wall material and a soft matrix at the scale of the bacteria. No text, letters, numerals, labels, worded arrows, scale bars, watermarks, chemical formulae, invented microscope or screen interfaces. No person, face or hand in either frame, and no body. No medical horror — no blood, no wound, no inflamed or ulcerated tissue. No black background, no blue neon glow, no false-colour electron-micrograph cliché, no stock DNA helix. No collage of separate plates, no split panel, no inset. The wide infographic is never a wider version of the card frame and the card frame is never cut from it — two frames, two worlds. No forked or spherical cell standing in for a rod: the morphology in field 3 is the truth of this frame.

Four honesty limits, as binding as the bans above:

- The arrivals must never be shown **colonising** — no arrival cell dividing, spreading or building a patch in the resident field. One takes a place; the rest pass. That is what the trial found.
- The arrivals must never be drawn as the **cause** of the outcome in beat three. The variety at the end belongs to the residents.
- Nothing may depict a **health result in a body** — no calmed tissue, no healing, no before-and-after person. The trial's primary outcome was not significant; the inflammatory-protein finding is a secondary one and cannot be pictured as a cure.
- The pasteurised, heat-treated version is **not** in this frame at all. No dead or greyed-out cells, no ruined field, nothing that reads as a warning. The live ferment is the only thing depicted.
