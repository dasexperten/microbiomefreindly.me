## 2. The mechanism in one clause (from the `answer` field)

Fermentable fibre — the kind your own enzymes cannot break but your colon bacteria can — is what those bacteria eat, while viscous gel-forming and coarse fibres pass through doing other jobs entirely, and the fermentation effect levels off rather than rising forever.

## 3. The organism or structure, with its real morphology in plain words

The subject is **not one species**. It is a stretch of colon lining where three physically different fibre bodies meet a mixed bacterial community, and the point of the frame is that they are not alike. Every shape below is either named in the article or is that organism's ordinary textbook form. None of it may be redrawn as something else.

**The living bodies:**

- ***Bifidobacterium*** — short rods that split at one end into a **Y**, sometimes into a small extra branch; irregular, slightly club-ended, no flagella, no spores. These are the cells that multiply in the frame, because they are the genus the pooled trials moved most. **Never spheres.**
- ***Lactobacillus*** — slender straight rods with rounded ends, plainly longer than they are wide, often lying end to end in short pairs or short chains. Not forked, not round, no spores.
- **Ordinary resident rods** — plain straight rods with rounded ends, various lengths, filling the background crowd so the frame reads as a community and not as two named species alone.

**The non-living bodies — these carry the whole argument and must be three visibly different things:**

- **Fermentable fibre** — fine, **branched chains**: a soft backbone with short side-branches coming off it, like a frayed thread, loose and open enough that a cell can plainly get at it. This is the fructan / galacto-oligosaccharide / resistant-starch class. It is the only one that gets eaten, and it must look edible: soft, open, approachable.
- **Viscous fibre** — a **smooth translucent gel ribbon or sheet**, thick and slick, holding its shape, no branches and no frayed ends. Gel-forming beta-glucan or psyllium. It slides past and is never broken open by any cell.
- **Coarse insoluble fibre** — an **angular, hard-edged plant-wall fragment**: a flat flake with straight broken edges and visible internal cell-wall structure, like a bran particle. Rigid, dry-looking, nothing soft about it. It passes through intact and is never eaten.

**The ground they sit on:** a soft, semi-transparent mucus layer lying over the rounded, finger-like folds of the colon lining. A living surface, not a hard crust and not a tunnel. It stays whole and ungrazed in every beat: the only evidence that the community turns on the lining when fibre is missing is in mice (`s8`), so the frame neither thickens nor thins it.

Morphology is truth (`docs/IMAGE_PROMPT.md`, rule 3). Rods stay rods, *Bifidobacterium* stays forked, and the three fibre bodies stay three genuinely different materials — soft and branched, smooth and gel-like, angular and hard. If a viewer cannot tell them apart at a glance, the frame has failed at the one thing it exists to say.

## 4. The three-beat process — first, next, outcome

Taken from the article's H2 skeleton, in one reading path:

1. **First — three kinds of fibre arrive together.** The branched chains, the gel ribbon and the angular bran flake land on the mucus layer side by side, in the same crowd of cells. Nothing has happened yet. The eye should register: *these are three different materials, not three helpings of one thing.*
2. **Next — only one of them is a meal.** The forked and rod-shaped cells crowd onto the branched chains and take them apart; the chains visibly thin and shorten as the cells work them. The gel ribbon slides on untouched, still smooth and whole. The angular flake passes untouched, still hard-edged and whole. Two of the three bodies leave the frame exactly as they entered it.
3. **Outcome — a fed community, up to a point.** Where the chains were consumed, the forked cells are more numerous (`s2`) and the mucus layer beneath them is still whole. And beside that, the ceiling: as the incoming quantity of branched chains keeps rising, what comes out of the fermentation rises and then stops rising.

The eye should read **three fibres → one is eaten → a fed community with a ceiling** in one path. That last half-beat is the argument of the title: more is a lever with a stop on it, not a dial.

## 5. The single sourced number, shown by scale or quantity

**The plateau: resistant-starch effects were dose-dependent but plateaued at 35 g a day.**
Source: Deehan E. C. et al., *Cell Host & Microbe*, 2020 — doi `10.1016/j.chom.2020.01.006`, PMID `32004499` (article source `s3`).

Show it as **proportion between quantities, never as a printed figure**: in the outcome beat, three successively larger heaps of branched fermentable chains sit in the frame, and beside each is the quantity of fermentation product it yielded. Heap one yields a small quantity. Heap two is markedly larger and yields a markedly larger quantity. Heap three is larger again — and yields a quantity that is plainly, unmistakably *the same* as heap two's. The rise stops while the input keeps growing. The gram value itself is not encoded — the frame carries the plateau that the 35 g figure marks, not a quantity a reader could count as grams. Nothing is labelled, nothing is measured, no axis, no bar, no chart furniture; the whole statement is carried by how much of each thing is visibly there.

**Honest note on the other number.** The article's headline figure is 25 g to 29 g a day (Reynolds A. et al., *The Lancet*, 2019 — doi `10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31809-9`, PMID `30638909`, article source `s1`). That is a **mass eaten per day**, and there is no way to show a gram count with microscopic bodies without printing a numeral, which this lane forbids. It therefore stays in the text where its source marker can follow it, and it must not be smuggled into the frame as a scale, a spoon, a plate, a heap-that-means-29, or any other quantity a reader could try to count as grams. **Only one number is in the image, and it is the plateau shown as a proportion.** No second figure — not the 64 trials, not the 2099 adults, not the 5–8 hour delay, not the 0–12 intolerance score, not the 45.1 g.
