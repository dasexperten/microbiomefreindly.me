**2. The mechanism in one clause (from the `answer` field)**

Fibre-rich plants and fermented foods feed a community that comes back near to what it was in about a month and a half after the course, while nine common species are still not found in most of the men at day 180.

**3. The organism or structure, with its real morphology in plain words**

This frame is not one species. It is a mixed gut community sitting on plant fibre, and the fibre is as much the subject as the cells. Every shape below is either named in the work behind the piece or is that organism's ordinary textbook form. Nothing here is invented, and nothing here may be redrawn as something else:

- **The ground: plant fibre.** Pale, matte, slightly translucent strands and sheets — the remains of plant cell walls after chewing and stomach acid. Ragged-edged flat sheets and loose bundles of fine parallel strands, laid in an open lattice with gaps between them, not a solid mat. This is what the bacteria settle on and eat. It is fibre, never a bowl, never a plate, never a recognisable vegetable.
- **A thin mucus film** lying over the gut lining beneath the lattice — soft and translucent, holding the whole field. Beneath it the lining rises in a rank of finger-shaped folds.
- **Plain straight rods with rounded ends** — the ordinary residents, the bulk of the population, lying along and between the fibre strands. Some with fine hair-like tails, most without.
- **Forked cells** — *Bifidobacterium*: short rods that split at one end into a Y, sometimes into a small branch. Never spheres.
- **Short plump rods, and small round cells in short chains of three to six** — the lactic acid bacteria of fermented foods in their ordinary form. They arrive from outside and lie among the residents; they are not drawn larger or brighter than the residents, because they are not the heroes of the outcome.

No spores swelling a cell end, no spirals, no filaments, no biofilm sheet, no capsule-shaped body of any kind: none of those belong in this frame.

**4. The three-beat process — first, next, outcome**

1. **First:** the fibre lattice lies almost bare. The four-day course has stripped the field; only scattered cells remain in the mucus, and most of the strand surface has nothing on it. Emptiness is the state the reader starts from.
2. **Next:** fresh fibre arrives and the cells settle on it. Rods, forks and the short chains from fermented food spread along the strands and out into the gaps — the field visibly filling from the fibre outward, over roughly six weeks. This is the working middle of the frame and should carry the most detail.
3. **Outcome:** the field is crowded again and reads at a glance as it did before — except that nine places in it stay empty. Recovery is real, and it is incomplete.

The eye must walk that path in one pass: bare lattice → cells spreading along the fibre → crowded field with nine gaps.

**5. The single sourced number, shown by scale or quantity**

**Nine.** Nine species present in all 12 men before the course were still undetectable in most of them 180 days later — Palleja A. et al., *Nature Microbiology*, 2018 (`s1` in the article, doi 10.1038/s41564-018-0257-9, PMID 30349083).

Shown as **nine visibly empty places** in the refilled field — nine clear pockets on the fibre where a cell plainly used to sit, countable without effort in an otherwise crowded frame. Nine exactly: not eight, not a suggestion of "some". The number is never printed, never labelled, never written anywhere in the pixels. Quantity is the whole of it.

No medical horror — no blood, no wound, no inflamed or ulcerated tissue. No black background, no blue neon glow, no false-colour electron-micrograph cliché, no stock DNA helix. No collage of separate plates, no split panel, no inset. The wide infographic is never a wider version of the card frame and the card frame is never cut from it — two frames, two worlds. No spherical *Bifidobacterium*: the morphology in field 3 is the truth of this frame.

Two honesty limits that are as binding as the bans above:

- The nine empty places must stay empty in the last beat — nothing may show food closing them, because no study says it does.
- The fermented-food cells must not be drawn as the agent that refills the field, because the trial behind them was run in healthy adults with no antibiotics involved.
