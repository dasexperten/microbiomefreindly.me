## 2. The mechanism in one clause (from the `answer` field)

Anaerobic bacteria on the back of the tongue and under the gums make the volatile sulfur compounds that are the smell — above the food pipe and not below it — and in a breath clinic of 491 patients the mouth explained 87 % of cases and the digestive tract 1 %.

## 3. The organism or structure, with its real morphology in plain words

The frame is a **surface and the anaerobic community living in it**, not a single named species. The honest subject of this piece is a place — the back third of the tongue — because that is where the argument is won.

- **The tongue's dorso-posterior surface.** A fissured, papillated surface: soft rounded and thread-like papillae standing close together with deep narrow grooves between them. Not smooth, not spiked, not a hard crust.
- **The coating that sits on it.** A thick, soft, semi-opaque layer of shed epithelial cells, saliva sediment and food debris packed down into those grooves. It reads as sediment settled into a fissured landscape, not as slime poured over it.
- **The oxygen line inside the coating.** Near the top the coating is aerated and open; a short way down oxygen has run out. This is the single most important spatial fact in the frame and it must be legible as depth: lit and open above, dim and closed below.
- **The anaerobes in the dead layer.** Short, plump rods with rounded ends, roughly bean- or barrel-shaped and some almost round, about one to two micrometres long — non-motile, lying singly and in loose clusters, fringed with fine hair-like fimbriae, resting in the wet film rather than swimming in it. This is the true morphology of *[Porphyromonas gingivalis](/bacteria/porphyromonas-gingivalis/)*, which the article names as a major producer of both gases [s11], and it is the shape the frame carries. **No spores** — this organism forms none. **No flagella, no swimming tails.** Not long thin rods, not chains, not grape-bunch spheres.
- **The periodontal pocket.** The same anaerobic condition in a second place: a narrow wet groove between tooth surface and gum, the same short rods resting in the same thin film, deeper down where the air stops.

Morphology is truth (IMAGE_PROMPT, rules that outlive any engine, 3). Rods stay rods here, they stay short and plump rather than slender, and nothing in this frame swims.

**On *Helicobacter pylori*.** The article discusses it and rules it out as a settled cause [s7] [s8]. It therefore does **not** appear in either frame. If a spiral cell were drawn anywhere, the image would argue the opposite of the piece.

## 4. The three-beat process — first, next, outcome

Taken from the article's H2 skeleton, in one reading path:

1. **First — the coating builds and the oxygen runs out.** Shed cells, saliva sediment and debris settle into the grooves between the papillae until the lower part of the layer is closed off from the air. The upper coating is still open and lit; beneath it the world goes dim.
2. **Next — the anaerobes work on protein.** In that closed lower layer the short plump rods break down the sulfur-carrying parts of the shed cells' own protein and release the sulfur as gas. The substrate is the reader's own dead cells, not food — that is what makes the mechanism uncomfortable and memorable.
3. **Outcome — the gas lifts off the tongue's back third into mouth air.** Fine plumes rise out of the grooves and off the surface, upward and forward into open mouth air. They leave from **above** the food pipe. Below the tongue, the throat and everything beneath it recede into calm, quiet, unpopulated depth — nothing is being made down there.

The eye should read **coating settles → sulfur is stripped in the dark layer → gas lifts upward and forward**, and should finish with a clear sense of *up here, not down there*. That direction is the whole argument of the piece.
