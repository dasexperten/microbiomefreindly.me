You have heard the promise. Three days of juice, or broth, or a powdered gut protocol, and your microbiome starts over. Clean slate. New you.

Here is the verdict, and it is a split one. Partly true. Three days is genuinely enough to move a gut community. It is nowhere near enough to reset one.

The evidence for the first half is good. In a study published in Nature in twenty fourteen, ten volunteers ate two extreme diets, one entirely animal, one entirely plant, for five days each. The community moved, and it moved fast. Differences showed up one day after the food reached the gut. Bile-tolerant bacteria rose on the animal diet. The plant fermenters fell. A controlled feeding study in Science had clocked the same speed: composition changed within twenty-four hours of a diet switch.

So yes, three days will show up in a stool sample.

Now the other direction, from that same Nature paper. The volunteers' gut communities reverted to their original structure two days after the diet ended. Two days. And the deeper layer never moved at all. In the Science study, the broad configuration a person sits in stayed stable across the full ten days, and it tracked years of eating, not one week of it.

The number that settles this comes from counting individual strains. In thirty-seven American adults sampled across five years, on average sixty per cent of the roughly two hundred strains in the gut were still there at the end. The authors' curve suggests most strains stay for decades. Your gut is not a slate. It is a long-settled neighbourhood.

If a juice fast cannot reset a gut, what can? Antibiotics come closest and still fall short. Twelve healthy men took four days of three last-resort antibiotics. Their communities crashed, then rebuilt to near baseline in about six weeks. But nine species that every one of them carried beforehand were still missing in most of them six months later. That is damage plus regrowth. It is not a clean slate.

Reset is the wrong word, because there is no factory state to return to. Diet changes your gut quickly, and it holds the change for exactly as long as you hold the diet. Three days is a reading, not a renovation.
