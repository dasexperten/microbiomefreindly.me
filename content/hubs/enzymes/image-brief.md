**4. Morphology, in words (truth, Magnus):**
- Dextran: a long chain of glucose units — small six-sided sugar rings linked one after another, mostly in a straight line with occasional short side branches. Several chains cross loosely into a web.
- Dextranase: a compact folded protein, knobbly and irregular like a tightly crumpled ribbon, not a smooth ball, with one clear groove across its face. The sugar chain lies in that groove, and the one link inside it is the link being cut. A protein is not a cell: no membrane, no nucleus, no tails, no face.
- Mouth streptococci (second frame): small round cells joined in chains, like strings of beads, held inside the web of dextran chains.
- The first frame shows no bacteria — only the enzyme and its substrate.
- The hub also names papain, bromelain, lysozyme and glucose oxidase; they are left out so one enzyme walks one path. No fruit, no bursting cells.
