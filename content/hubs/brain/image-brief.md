## The brief

**1. Slug (ASCII, language-neutral):** `brain`
File names: `brain-preview` · `brain-hero`.

**2. Article title (EN) + one-line summary:** "Gut–brain axis: how bacteria reach the brain without moving" — the gut and its microbes talk to the brain through the vagus nerve, hormones from gut cells, immune messengers and bacterial metabolites; the routes are real, their size in human mood is still being measured.

**3. Organism / structure in the preview:** the enteric nervous system — the mesh of neurons in the intestinal wall the hub opens with. The bacteria never leave the gut; this net is part of the wiring they use.

**4. Morphology, in words (truth, Magnus):**
- The enteric nerve net: a wide-meshed, irregular, many-sided net lying flat in the intestinal wall. Its knots are small clusters of plump rounded nerve cell bodies; each knot is joined to the next by a smooth, cable-like bundle of fine fibres. It is not a starburst of single brain-style neurons and not a glowing circuit.
- The wall it lies on: long, thin, spindle-shaped muscle cells packed side by side in one direction.
- The gut lining (second frame): a single row of tall column-shaped cells. Among them one enteroendocrine cell — the hormone-releasing cell of the lining — shaped like a flask: a narrow tip reaching the gut space, a broad base packed with tiny round granules.
- The vagus nerve (second frame): a fine nerve fibre ending touches the base of that cell and joins a smooth, cable-like nerve bundle that runs away.
- Bacteria: the hub names no species for this route, so they are generic — short rods with rounded ends and small round cocci. No species is implied.
- Metabolites (short-chain fatty acids, tryptophan derivatives, GABA): tiny simple molecules, each a small cluster of a few linked beads. Never a helix.

**5. Preview mood (one line, warm science):** a quiet net of nerve knots lying across a soft, ribbed muscle wall under silvery overcast light — calm, even, nothing glowing; one knot carries the only lime note.

**6. Hero mechanism (the answer, one clause):** gut microbes talk to the brain without moving — their metabolites are sensed by the hormone-releasing cells of the gut lining, and the vagus nerve carries the message away.

**7. Three beats (first → next → outcome):** (1) bacteria in the gut space release small metabolite molecules; (2) a flask-shaped enteroendocrine cell in the lining takes the signal in at its tip and releases granules at its base; (3) outcome: the nerve fibre under that base picks the signal up and it runs away along the vagus nerve, out of the frame — while the bacteria stay where they started.
