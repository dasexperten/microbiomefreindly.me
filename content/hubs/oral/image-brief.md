## The brief

**2. Article title (EN) + one-line summary:** "The mouth microbiome: plaque, caries and gums, explained" — on tooth enamel the mouth's bacteria build plaque, a structured biofilm held together by sugars they make themselves; in balance it protects, but frequent sugar lets acid-making species take over and enamel dissolves.

**3. Organism / structure in the preview:** plaque at its start — a young biofilm mound on enamel, the only surface in the body that never sheds, as the hub puts it.

**4. Morphology, in words (truth, Magnus):**
- Enamel, drawn only as a cell-scale mineral landscape: a hard, pale plain built of tightly packed rod-like crystal bundles whose rounded ends show as a fine, regular scale pattern; a thin clear film of saliva lies over it. No tooth outline, no mouth.
- The pioneers: small round cells joined in short chains, stuck to the saliva film. The hub names no species; this is the ordinary shape of the mouth's early settlers and acid-makers, and no species is implied.
- The later arrivals: slender rods attaching onto the pioneers.
- The matrix: a glossy, translucent, sticky sugar gel (glucans) wrapping the whole colony into one mound — the walls of the city.
- Sugar (second frame): small sugar molecules, each a pair of linked rings (sucrose).
- Acid (second frame): a soft haze gathered where the mound meets the enamel.
- Dissolving enamel (second frame): under the mound the neat scale pattern softens, the rod ends pit and roughen into a shallow hollow — chemistry, not a hole, not decay drama.

**5. Preview mood (one line, warm science):** a small young mound of bead-chains wrapped in glossy sugar gel, rising from a pale mineral plain at soft peach dawn with light coming low from the left; the gel's glossy rim carries the only gold.

**6. Hero mechanism (the answer, one clause):** when sugar is frequent, acid-making bacteria take over the plaque biofilm and the acid they make dissolves the enamel beneath it.
