**6. Hero mechanism (the answer, one clause) — Magnus, 2026-09-16**

Heat-killed *Akkermansia* lying in the mucus against the gut lining nudged the lining's hormone cells into releasing more of the body's own GLP-1 after a glucose drink — an exploratory signal — while whole-body insulin sensitivity, the one thing the trial was built to test, did not move.

**7. Three beats (first → next → outcome)**

1. A dense drift of heat-killed oval cells — whole, unbroken, perfectly still — settles into the mucus blanket pressed against a short row of lining cells.
2. Where the mucus is thinnest, small protein molecules from those inert cells reach one hormone-making cell of the lining (an L cell), and it lets out a small burst of hormone packets.
3. Outcome: the burst stays a local event — beyond it, the wide field of surrounding tissue cells lies exactly as it was, unchanged. A nudge, not an effect.
