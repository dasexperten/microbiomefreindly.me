**1. Slug (ASCII, language-neutral)**
chewing-gum-enzymes-oral-acetaldehyde

**2. Article title (EN) + one-line summary**
"The carcinogen your own mouth makes out of ordinary sugar" — microbes cultured from the saliva
of head-and-neck cancer patients turned sugar and alcohol into acetaldehyde, a Group 1
carcinogen; the sticky plaque shell protected the ones hiding inside it until added enzymes
dissolved that shell. Laboratory work only, no human trial.

**3. Organism / structure in the preview**
The dental biofilm itself — the sticky plaque shell — with the mouth organisms of this study held
inside it: a Candida yeast, streptococci, and the two gum-disease anaerobes. The structure is the
subject; the cells are the inhabitants.

**4. Morphology, in words (truth — do not invent shapes)**
Streptococci: small spheres, in short chains and beaded strings, never single scattered dots.
Candida: larger oval cells, some with a smaller bud attached at one end, a few in short strings.
Fusobacterium nucleatum: slender spindle-shaped rods, tapering to a point at both ends.
Porphyromonas gingivalis: short blunt rods, almost egg-shaped, single or in pairs.
All of them embedded in a translucent, slightly stringy film that clings to a pale, faintly
ridged hard surface — tooth enamel seen from very close, glass-smooth and warm, not white-bright.
Scale cue: the enamel surface runs across the frame with its shallow ridges readable, so the eye
understands that the beaded chains sitting in the film are far smaller than one of those ridges.

**5. Preview mood (one line, warm science)**

with one place near the edge where it has thinned and let the light through to the cells beneath.
Ground ivory #F1EADC; cells and film in navy #1B3856, teal #178B7A and green #2F7D55; a single
small gold #C7A24B note where the light catches the thinned place. No people, no text, no

**6. Hero mechanism (the answer, one clause) — Magnus, 2026-09-16**
Mouth microbes turn sugar and alcohol into acetaldehyde, a Group 1 carcinogen; a peptide stopped the output, but the
cells sheltered inside the sticky plaque shell survived until added enzymes dissolved that shell. Laboratory dishes only.

**7. Three beats (first → next → outcome)**
1. On a pale enamel plain, a sticky translucent shell holds chains of small round streptococci and budding oval yeast
   cells; plain sugar grains reach it, and a faint haze of acetaldehyde lifts from the film.
2. The peptide washes over: the haze stops and most of the chained cells lie pale, whole and inert — but a few round
   cells deep under the intact shell stay alive.
3. Outcome: enzymes dissolve the shell into loose, parting strands, the sheltered survivors lie exposed, and the peptide
   finishes them — the enamel plain is left quiet and bare.

**8. The one number (keyFacts, source id)**
Protegrin-1 with bean gum killed only **91.37 %** of the biofilm-forming streptococcal colonies; dextranase and mutanase
were needed for the rest (`s1`, Molecular Therapy: Oncology 2026). Shown only as proportion: of exactly thirty-five
round cells in the chains at the peptide stage, three stay alive, all tucked deep under the unbroken shell. Never printed.
