Here's something I didn't expect to be saying about a biscuit.

Acetaldehyde is a Group 1 human carcinogen. That's the top category, the same shelf as tobacco smoke. And it turns out your own mouth can make it, on the spot, out of ordinary sugar.

That's the finding in a new paper from the University of Pennsylvania School of Dental Medicine, published this year in Molecular Therapy: Oncology. The researchers didn't use tidy laboratory strains. They took saliva from patients with head and neck cancer and grew what was actually living in it — a yeast, some streptococci, and two anaerobes that dentists know well, Fusobacterium nucleatum and Porphyromonas gingivalis.

Then they fed them sugar, and fed them alcohol, and measured what came out. And what came out was acetaldehyde. More sugar, more carcinogen — up to a point. It peaked around a hundred millimolar and then dropped off, probably because that much sugar stresses the cell. Here's the detail I'd underline: the streptococci made it with no alcohol present at all. Straight from sugar. So this isn't only a drinking story.

Then they tried to switch it off. They used an antimicrobial peptide called protegrin-1. Against a bug with no biofilm, it worked beautifully. Against the biofilm builders, it didn't. And that's the mechanism worth carrying home, because you can picture it. Biofilm bacteria live inside a shell of sticky sugar polymer. Spraying an antiseptic on that is like spraying disinfectant on a zipped-up tent and calling the inside clean.

So they added two enzymes that chew through the shell — dextranase and mutanase. Open the tent, and the peptide finished the job. Acetaldehyde went to undetectable. The delivery idea is a chewing gum with those enzymes inside.

Now the part that has to be said out loud. This is a dish. Monocultures, in vitro, a handful of isolates. They measured acetaldehyde, not DNA damage. Nobody was treated. No cancer was prevented. There's nothing to buy here.

But the mechanism points somewhere honest and cheap. Fewer sugar hits across the day, less alcohol, and break up that biofilm morning and night. Same supply chain — and those already exist.
