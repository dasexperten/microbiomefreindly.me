Two people buy the same probiotic. One loses a few pounds and swears by it. The other feels nothing and decides the whole thing is a scam. Here's the strange part: they're both right. And a trial published in Gut Microbes in 2026 shows why.

Researchers in Seoul took 120 overweight adults and, for 12 weeks, gave half of them a placebo and half a heat-killed preparation of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LRCC5282. Heat-killed, so dead cells. Nothing here is about live bacteria moving in.

The headline result? Nothing. Body fat on the scan didn't differ. Weight fell about 1.3 kilos on the product and about 0.9 on placebo, a gap that's easily chance. Leptin, cholesterol, waist: no difference anywhere.

Then the researchers went back to the stool samples taken before day one and split everyone by how many kinds of bacteria they were carrying. And in the low-diversity half, the story flipped. Weight fell about 2 kilos on the product against about 1 on placebo. BMI fell more. Blood leptin, the hormone fat tissue makes, fell more. Their stool carried more acetate and butyrate, the fatty acids gut bacteria make from fibre. And the community itself had shifted toward Faecalibacterium, Alistipes and Christensenellaceae. In the high-diversity half, same sachet, essentially nothing happened.

Why? Ecology. A crowded gut has every seat taken, so a newcomer, even a dead one, changes little. A thinned-out gut has room to move.

Now the caveats, out loud. The primary endpoint, body fat, failed everywhere, including in the responders. The diversity split came after the main result was null, and each group had only about 25 people. Nobody tracked the strain, nobody measured diet precisely, and nobody was followed past week 12. And every author works for the company that owns the strain.

So what do you take home? Don't judge a probiotic by your friend. A flat average can hide people it helped, and a glowing story can hide people it didn't. This trial says the dividing line might be how diverse your gut was before you started. It doesn't tell you which side of that line you're on. That answer is still being collected.
